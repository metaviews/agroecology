**Vibe Coding for Agroecology — A Right to Repair Tool, Built with M3**

Most "AI for ag" tools are built by engineers. What if a farmer could build their own tools, with AI as a coding partner?

The answer: an **Equipment Maintenance Assistant** built fully with M3 for a small farm near Almonte, Ontario. JSON in, get a maintenance calendar, parts list (OEM + aftermarket sourcing), troubleshooting guide, handoff doc, and printable shop card. Static HTML, file-based, no SaaS, no platform. Fork it, edit it, run offline on a USB stick.

Canonical demo: three real machines from the farm — a 1967-1975 John Deere 1120 (5,335 hrs, vintage DIY), a 2022 Polaris Ranger EV (electric, dealer-locked but commodity batteries), and a 2021 Sunward SWE18UF excavator (geography-locked, not DRM-locked). Three right to repair stories, one tool.

The frame is right to repair: parts list has a "source independently" column, maintenance calendar flags DIY-able intervals from dealer-only steps, handoff doc closes with "leave the next operator better than you found the machine."

The wider fight: the January 2023 John Deere / AFBF MOU, Colorado's 2024 ag right to repair law, Canada's Bill C-244. The laws say farmers can repair; this tool says they can also document, share, and own the knowledge.

The four "why M3" claims — domain translation, pedagogical scaffolding, sovereignty by design, customization at the edge — are on the process page with concrete "M3 said X, I changed it to Y, because Z" examples. The M3 / owner split is the receipt, not a footnote.

Built with M3 (MiniMax-M3) and a farmer: agroecology, right to repair framing, real farm data on one side; synthesis, code, design scaffolding, illustration on the other.
